# Restaurant month-end close checklist

A restaurant month-end close has 10 steps, done in order. After the bank and
card accounts tie to their printed statements, the work that is specific to a
restaurant comes next: proving that point-of-sale sales equal the deposits from
cards, cash and delivery apps, accounting for tips and sales tax collected for
others, and counting inventory so cost of sales is real rather than the
month's purchases.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `checklist.md` | The 10 steps with tick boxes. Print it, or copy it into a task tool or a GitHub issue each month. |
| `close-tracker.csv` | One row per step with Owner, Due day, Done and Notes columns. Save a copy per month and change Done to Yes as each step finishes. |

## Why the sales tie-out comes first

A restaurant's deposits never match the POS total directly. Card batches arrive
net of processing fees, delivery apps pay out net of commission, tips are
included in the card batch but belong to staff, and tax is included but belongs
to the government. Step 3 starts at the POS monthly report and works down to
the deposits, listing each difference by name. A difference with no name is
the thing to look for before anything else is posted.

Due days count working days after month end. Counts in step 7 have to happen on
the last day of the month, not when the bookkeeper gets to it.

See the chart of accounts for restaurants in

## FAQ

### Why don't my restaurant's deposits match the point-of-sale total?

Deposits are net of card processing fees and delivery app commissions, they
exclude cash kept for tips or change, and they arrive a day or more after the
sale. The POS total is gross. The tie-out lists each of these differences so
what is left unexplained is small.

### Are tips income for the restaurant?

Tips left by customers belong to the staff. Tips collected on cards are held in
a liability account until paid out, so they never appear in the restaurant's
sales or profit.

### How often should a restaurant count inventory?

At least once a month, on the last day, so that cost of sales equals opening
inventory plus purchases minus closing inventory. Many restaurants also count
the highest-cost items, such as proteins and liquor, weekly.
