# Small business month-end close checklist

A month-end close for a small business has 13 steps, done in this order: get
every statement in, reconcile each bank and card account to its printed closing
balance, pair transfers, clear receivables and payables, post accruals,
prepaids, payroll from the register, depreciation and the loan interest split,
tie out sales tax, review the P&L against last month, scan the balance sheet,
then lock the month and send the reports. The reconciliations come early
because every later entry depends on the cash being right.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `checklist.md` | The 13 steps with tick boxes. Print it, or copy it into a task tool or a GitHub issue each month. |
| `close-tracker.csv` | One row per step with Owner, Due day, Done and Notes columns. Open it in Excel or Google Sheets, save a copy per month and change Done to Yes as each step finishes. |

## How to use the tracker

1. Make a copy for the month, for example `close-2026-09.csv`.
2. Change Owner to real names. In a one-person business that is you for every
   row except step 11, which works better with a second pair of eyes.
3. Due day counts working days after month end. A business that closes within
   10 working days is doing well; most of that time is waiting for statements.
4. Don't tick step 13 until steps 1 to 12 say Yes.

## Why the order matters

Steps 2 and 3 prove the cash. Steps 4 to 10 adjust for things the bank
statement can't show: sales and bills not yet paid, costs that cover several
months, and payroll withholdings that never left the bank. Steps 11 and 12
catch what slipped through. If step 2 fails, stop there: a balance that won't
tie to the printed statement means a missing, doubled or misread line, and
every later step would build on it.
